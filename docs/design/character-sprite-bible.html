Character Sprite Bible

The reference for replacing the flat player rectangles with real 16 px characters. It covers the character design screen players see before every new dungeon save, where that screen sits in the flow, the sprite and data spec, and a first catalogue of cosmetics. Every sprite on this page is drawn live from the pixel data in this file, so this page is the source of truth an implementing agent can copy from.

AT A GLANCE

What this page decides

16 × 24 frame16 px wide like every tile, plus 8 px of headroom above for tall hair and hats. Drawn at 2× (32 × 48 screen px).
4 choicesBody (guy or girl), skin tone (6), hair style (6), hair colour (8). That gives 576 base looks. No cosmetics on this screen.
Per savePlayers design a character for each new dungeon save. It is locked for that save. Continuing a save skips the screen.
Party colourAssigned by the server by join order. It tints the base tunic and draws a ring under the feet, so teammates stay readable whatever they wear.
Palette swapArt is authored once with key colours, then recoloured at runtime into a cached canvas per player. No sprite is drawn per combination.
5 cosmetic slotsHat, cloak, outfit dye, weapon trail, torch flame. They never change stats and are never lost on death.

Screen rules

      
    
        
  • Opens with a random look, or with the player's last-used look from local storage if there is one. The screen is never blank.
    • 
        
  • Hair styles are not gated by body. Every style works on both bodies.
    • 
        
  • The name is prefilled from the lobby and can be edited here. The same 24-character limit applies.
    • 
        
  • Party colour is shown but not chosen. The server assigns it by join order (Ember, Moss, Azure, Plum), so it appears as a chip, not a control.
    • 
        
  • Keyboard: Tab between groups, arrows inside a group, Enter to confirm. R randomizes.
    • 
        
  • "Enter the hub" sends setAppearance. The button shows "Entering…" until the server spawns the player, or shows the server's error inline.
    • 
        
  • "Back" returns to the lobby and gives up the reserved party slot.
    • 
        
  • At phone width the stage stacks above the options, and the large preview shrinks to 6×.
    • 
      

    

    

      
Why only four choices

      
Each choice maps to one palette ramp or one layer. That keeps the art load small: 2 bodies and 6 hair layers, each in 3 directions, cover all 576 looks. Everything else is a cosmetic, earned in the hub and layered on top. Eye colour, face shapes and body sizes were left out on purpose. At 16 px an eye is a single dark pixel, so those options would not read in play.

      
Tunic colour is not a choice. If players picked tunic colours, two teammates could match and stop being readable in a dark corridor. The party colour handles identity, and outfit dyes from the shop override the tunic later. The ring under the feet always keeps the party colour.

    

  







  

    FLOW · WHERE THE SCREEN LIVES
    
Name, room, then character

    
Recommendation: make character design its own screen, shown right after the server accepts the room. Don't fold it into the lobby. Three reasons:

    
    
      
  • Continuing a save skips it. The server knows whether this player already has a character in this room's save, and the lobby does not. Designing first would waste the player's effort whenever they turn out to be continuing.
    • 
      
  • The room can refuse you. A bad code, a full party, or a run in progress should fail before the player spends a minute on hair.
    • 
      
  • The lobby stays short. It is two fields and two buttons today, and that works well on a phone.
    •

    SPEC · SPRITES
    
Frame, layers and palette

    
These rules let the characters sit on the existing flagstones without looking pasted in. They follow the conventions already in assets/sprites/source/dungeon.tiles.json: 16 px per world tile, drawn at 2×, one character per pixel, light from the top.

  


  

    

      
      

        Rows 0–7: headroom (tall hair, hats)
        Rows 8–23: body
        Anchor (8, 23): bottom centre of the feet
        Collision circle, r = 0.35 tile (5.6 px)
      

    

    

      
Frame

      
    
        
  • Every character frame is 16 × 24 art pixels. The body uses the bottom 16 rows, and the top 8 rows are headroom that only hair and hats may use.
    • 
        
  • Anchor: the bottom-centre of pixel row 23 sits at position.y + PLAYER_RADIUS, centred on position.x. The silhouette is at most 12 px wide, which is about the 0.7-tile figure the renderer uses today.
    • 
        
  • Draw at the renderer's pixelsPerWorldUnit / 16 scale. At today's 32 px per tile that is 2×, so a frame is 32 × 48 on screen. Keep the scale an integer.
    • 
        
  • Characters are drawn sorted by position.y so overlapping teammates layer correctly. Names stay as fixed-size text, as they are now.
    • 
      

      
Art rules

      
    
        
  • A 1 px outline in #17120c (the renderer's current eye colour) around every layer's outer edge. Never use pure black.
    • 
        
  • Light comes from the top-left, matching the flagstones' lit top row. Use 3 tones per ramp (light, base, shadow) and no anti-aliasing.
    • 
        
  • Eyes are 1 × 2 px. The girl body adds one lash pixel at the outer top corner of each eye. That, the flared tunic and the narrower shoulders are the only body differences.
    • 
        
  • Left-facing frames are a horizontal mirror of right-facing ones. Asymmetric cosmetics such as the feathered cap flip with the sprite, which is fine.
    • 
      

    

  


  
Turnaround

6HatMay use the headroom rows.

          
7Held itemSword or bow, drawn at the hand point of the current frame (#129 adds the swing poses). The weapon trail is drawn here during a swing.

        
      

    

    

      
Animations

Walk4: stand, lift right, stand, lift left8 fps at walking speed. A lifted foot rises 1 row.

There are 3 directions per set: down, up and side. Left is the mirrored side. The hair and hat layers are authored once per direction and follow the body's head offset, so a walk frame that bobs the head 1 px carries the hair with it.

    

  


  
Palette ramps

  
The body and hair layers are authored once with key colours. At runtime each player's sprite is recoloured into an offscreen canvas and cached under the key body|skin|hairStyle|hairColor|party|dye|hat|cloak, so there are at most four at a time. Nothing else in the sheet may use a key colour, and a test fails the bake if one is found.

Authoring and bake

  
// assets/sprites/source/characters.tiles.json, same text format as dungeon.tiles.json,
// but with 16×24 frames and key-colour palette entries.
{
  "frameWidth": 16, "frameHeight": 24,
  "palette": { "0": "transparent", "k": "#17120c", "S": "#ff60ff", "H": "#00ffff", "c": "#ffff00", ... },
  "layers": {
    "body-guy-down":  { "y": 8, "rows": [ ...16 rows... ] },
    "hair-crop-down": { "y": 8, "rows": [ ... ] },          // y = first frame row the rows cover
    "hat-wizard-down": { "y": 1, "hairClipRow": 13, "rows": [ ... ] }
  }
}
// npm run build:sprites bakes both sheets. src/client/rendering/characters.ts lists every layer and where it sits
// (like tiles.ts does for the tilesheet), and builds per-player composites with getImageData key swaps.

  
The pixel rows for every body, hair style, hat and cloak on this page live in the SPRITES object in this page's script. Click "Copy all sprite data" in the creator to copy them as JSON. Use . for transparent here, and 0 in the repo's format. Body rows are relative to frame row 8 and other layers' y is relative to the body, so add 8 when porting.







  

    SPEC · DATA & PROTOCOL
    
Appearance on the wire

    
The server owns this state. The client sends ids from a fixed list, and the server rejects anything it does not recognise. The look is public: everyone in the party receives every player's appearance in snapshots.

  

  

    
// src/shared/constants/appearance.ts (shared by client and server)
export const BODY_TYPES = ["guy", "girl"] as const;
export const SKIN_TONES = ["porcelain", "fair", "olive",
  "tan", "brown", "deep"] as const;
export const HAIR_STYLES = ["crop", "spiky", "long",
  "ponytail", "bun", "shaved"] as const;
export const HAIR_COLORS = ["black", "brown", "chestnut",
  "blonde", "ginger", "silver", "azure", "moss"] as const;
export const PARTY_COLORS = ["ember", "moss", "azure", "plum"] as const;

// src/shared/types/core.ts
export interface PlayerAppearance {
  body: BodyType;
  skinTone: SkinTone;
  hairStyle: HairStyle;
  hairColor: HairColor;
}
export interface PlayerState {
  // ...existing fields
  appearance: PlayerAppearance | null;  // null = still designing
  partyColor: PartyColor;               // by join order, kept in the save
  cosmetics: EquippedCosmetics;         // later, #128
}

    

      
Messages

      
    
        
  • sessionJoined gains needsAppearance: boolean.
    • 
        
  • New client message setAppearance {"{"} appearance {"}"}. It is accepted only while the player's appearance is null. Once set, the look is locked for the life of the save.
    • 
        
  • Rejections: invalidAppearance (unknown id or wrong shape) and appearanceLocked (already set). Each comes with a readable message for the creator's error line.
    • 
        
  • Bump PROTOCOL_VERSION by one.
    • 
      

      
Persistence

      
    
        
  • The appearance is stored with the player's entry in the run save (#122), not on the profile, because a new save means a new character.
    • 
        
  • The client keeps the last look in localStorage only to prefill the creator next time. It is a convenience, never authority.
    • 
        
  • Content changes: if a saved id is later removed from a list, load the save with that field replaced by the list's first entry. Never refuse the save.
    • 
      

      
Tests

      
    
        
  • The server refuses an unknown id for each field, a missing field, extra fields, a second setAppearance, and a setAppearance before sessionJoined.
    • 
        
  • A player with a null appearance is not spawned and does not count as ready for the stairs.
    • 
        
  • The renderer composite cache returns the same canvas for the same key and a new one when a dye or hat changes.
    • 
        
  • Check with two real clients that each sees the other's look in the hub.
    •

Slots

      

        
SlotWhat it changesStatus

        
          
HatA layer over the hair. Each hat sets hairClipRow (hair above it is hidden), or hides all hair (hood), or keeps all of it (crown).in #128

          
CloakA behind layer and a back layer, with an optional pattern and animated hem pixels. Mostly seen from behind, which is how teammates see you walking ahead.new proposal

          
Outfit dyeReplaces the tunic ramp. The party ring keeps the party colour.in #128

          
Weapon trailThe arc drawn during a swing (#129), plus particles. It is the same size as the real hit arc, so it never misreports reach.in #128

          
Torch flameRecolours your torch's flame sprite and a faint halo. The lightmap radius and colour stay the same, so light remains a fair, shared resource.new proposal

        
      

    

    

      
Rules

      
    
        
  • Cosmetics never change stats, hitboxes, light radius or attack reach. A trail is drawn inside the real hit arc.
    • 
        
  • They unlock permanently on the profile, across all saves. They never drop in the dungeon and are never lost on death.
    • 
        
  • They can only be equipped in the hub, through equipCosmetic {"{"} slot, id | null {"}"}. The server checks ownership. In a dungeon the loadout is fixed.
    • 
        
  • They are public: they go in PlayerState.cosmetics, so everyone sees them.
    • 
        
  • Prices and the currency wait on #23. The "how to get" lines on the cards are proposals that mix shop items with milestone unlocks, so skilled play is shown off too.
    • 
        
  • Legendary items each have one small animation, such as crown embers or hem embers. Cap this at 2–3 animated pixels so a party of four never shimmers.
    •

STILL OPEN
Questions for the owner

  

    
Can a look be changed mid-save?Proposal: no. As an optional extra, a hub "mirror" could allow one free restyle per save, between runs only.

    
Is the party colour on the tunic right, or should it be a scarf or band so the tunic is free from the start?Proposal: use the tunic. It is the biggest area of colour and reads best in the dark, and dyes take over later.

    
Should cloaks and torch flames join the #128 slot list?They are new here. Both are cheap palette and layer work, and the cloak gives teammates walking behind you something to look at.

    
Are saves per room or per profile (#122)?This decides how the Continue list groups cards and whether one profile can hold several characters at once.

    
Should the body choice keep the labels "Guy" and "Girl"?They are used here as written in the brief. The data ids are guy and girl, so the labels can change later without touching saves.
