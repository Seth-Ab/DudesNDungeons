    Diagnosis

    Why every run feels the same

    The seed is not the problem. buildDungeon draws a fresh seed for every run. The problem is that every seed builds the same dungeon. These three maps come from today's real generateDungeon and vault-run.json, for seeds 11, 2024 and 90210:

    1

    template per landmark

    Entrance, puzzle, boss and exit each have exactly one template. Every run starts in Cinder Gate, solves the Hall of Paired Levers, fights the Ember Warden in Warden Crucible, and leaves by Ember Stair.

    ⅔

    of the world is never used

    placeRooms fills rooms left to right in reading order. In all three samples the rooms sit in rows 3 to 21 of 64, and the rest is solid rock. The start is always top-left and the boss always at the end of the second row.

    0

    branches, loops or choices

    The run is one chain: entrance, the slots in fixed order, then the exit. Nobody can choose a road, take a shortcut, get lost, or decide to split up. That removes most of what darkness and proximity voice are for.

    1

    lock, and any key opens it

    There is one locked door, in front of the exit room, and interact spends whatever key the player holds. That makes it impossible to have a second lock, such as a vault, without allowing sequence breaks.

    Research

    What the best dungeons do

    The games below are known for dungeons that stay interesting over hundreds of runs. Most of them rely on the same two ideas. First, the shape of the run is planned before any room is chosen. Second, rooms are hand-made, and the generator decides which ones appear and where. That also matches what GAMEDESCRIPTION.md asks for: dungeons "generated from large room templates".

    Spelunky

    Lays a 4×4 grid of rooms, walks a guaranteed solution path from a top-row entrance to a bottom-row exit, then gives each room a hand-made template that fits its role (drop, landing, corridor, off-path). Parts of each template are randomised as well.

    Walk the critical path on a coarse grid first, then fill rooms by role. A solvable route exists by construction.

    Spelunky generation write-up
    The Binding of Isaac

    Builds the floor plan on a grid, then assigns special rooms to dead ends, starting with the dead end farthest from the start. The boss gets the farthest one and the super secret room the next.

    Sort dead ends by distance and give the far ones the special rooms: vault, shrine, secret room. Distance makes them feel earned.

    Boris the Brave on Isaac
    Enter the Gungeon

    Each floor uses one of a handful of hand-made layout "flows" (4 to 8 per stage), each built around a feature such as a big loop or a multi-way fork. Rooms are hand-made. Combat rooms are spaced out with breather rooms, and chests sit behind longer one-way loops.

    Flows as content. A run's flow is chosen first and decides how many branches and loops it has and how the boss is locked.

    Boris the Brave on Gungeon
    Unexplored

    Joris Dormans' cyclic generation builds levels from loops instead of trees. A cycle runs from the entrance to the goal, and its two arcs are shaped by a cycle type, such as a lock on one arc with its key on the other. Loops cut down on dead ends and backtracking.

    Add loops on purpose. In the dark, two routes to the same room give a party a reason to split and a way to get separated.

    Boris the Brave on Unexplored
    Zelda, via Boss Keys

    Mark Brown's series charts each Zelda dungeon as a graph of rooms, locks and keys, and grades it by branching, player choice and backtracking. Ocarina of Time, for example, never lets you choose where to spend a key.

    Treat locks and keys as a graph, and check that graph on every run. Give each key a specific door so that a choice of where to spend it is real.

    Lock and key dungeons
    Brogue

    "Machines" are small authored set pieces dropped into generated levels: a key on an altar where picking it up sets off a hazard, or reward rooms where items sit on caged altars and taking one closes the cages on the rest.

    Altars that offer two skill scrolls and cage the second once the party takes one. The party has to agree on which, out loud.

    Brogue wiki: altars
    Dead Cells

    The team tried hand-made levels, found it too slow for a small team, and moved to a hybrid: hand-designed room chunks placed into a procedural layout. Runs feel familiar but are never the same.

    Keep hand-made templates for the rooms people remember (arenas, puzzles), and let procedural shapes (mazes, caverns) cover the filler.

    Deepnight: Dead Cells level design
    Deep Rock Galactic

    Missions can carry mutators. Warnings make a mission harder for a bigger payout; Anomalies change it in neutral or helpful ways. The game caps how many a mission can have, and they are shown before the mission starts.

    Omens: at most one warning and one anomaly per run, shown on the hub's quest board before the party readies up.

    DRG wiki: mutators
    Darkest Dungeon

    The light meter trades safety for profit. A bright torch means fewer ambushes and less stress; low light means more danger and better loot. Players snuff the torch on purpose in some rooms to raise the loot.

    The richest rooms are dark. Vaults get no lights, and a Dark Bargain lets the party put out its torches to improve a room's chests.

    Darkest Dungeon wiki: light meter
    Hades

    After a chamber is cleared, each exit door shows the reward behind it. A skull marks a harder room with a better reward. The player picks the reward before fighting for it.

    At a fork, the doors show what is beyond them. The party commits by standing on plates in front of a door, which reuses the hub's ready plates.

    Hades door symbols
    Keep Talking and Nobody Explodes

    The defuser sees the bomb and the experts see the manual. Neither can see what the other sees, so the game is mostly talking under pressure. Steel Crate's GDC talk covers designing puzzles to keep players talking.

    Puzzles where the clue is in one room and the controls are in another. Darkness and short voice range turn that into a real communication problem.

    The design

    Six rules for our dungeons

    These come from combining the research with the game's pillars: lighting, looting and fighting, proximity voice, and roguelite progression.

    Shape first, story second

    Walk the critical path, grow branches, add loops, then decide what each room is. Roles are assigned from the shape (distance, dead ends, loops), so the story always fits the map.

    Validate every run

    A run that breaks an invariant is re-rolled from seed and attempt number, never shipped. In 6,000 test seeds, 3 needed a second attempt and none needed a third.

    Content, not code

    Flows, biomes, bosses, puzzles, traps and omens are JSON under content/. A new flow or biome is a file and a restart, like a monster is today.

    Darkness is the price of greed

    Vaults and secret rooms have no lights. Corridors are lit sparsely and deliberately. A shrine can trade your light for better loot. Torches are always a choice.

    Every room asks the party something

    Fight, split up, talk, choose, hurry, or rest. A room that asks nothing is a corridor. Camps and shrines count, because they ask the party to agree.

    Scale with the party

    The server knows the party size when the gate opens. Plates never outnumber players plus ballast stones, the Split Seal needs two players, and threat budgets scale from 0.8× for one player to 1.4× for four.

    How a dungeon is built

    The seven-stage pipeline

    Each stage is a pure function of the seeded stream and the previous stage's output. Stage 1 reads the run seed directly, so a retry changes the layout but never the run's identity. Stages 2 to 7 read mixSeed(seed, attempt).

    1. 1

      Recipe

      Pick the flow (weighted, filtered by party size), the biome, a boss from the biome's pool, and up to one warning and one anomaly omen.

      New: generation/recipe.ts. Reads content/flows, content/biomes, content/omens.
    2. 2

      Layout

      Self-avoiding walk across a 6×4 grid of 16-tile cells for the critical path (entrance to boss to exit). Grow the flow's branches into free cells, then add loops between adjacent rooms that are at least three hops apart. The boss and exit never get loops or branches.

      Replaces chooseTemplates and placeRooms in dungeonGenerator.ts.
    3. 3

      Roles, locks and keys

      Lock the boss according to the flow: a key, seals or a split seal. Put the key holder on the farthest dead end. Give other far dead ends a vault, shrine or secret room. Put a camp at about 60% of the path. Fill the rest from the biome's weights, with no room kind twice in a row and no trap right after the entrance.

      New: generation/roles.ts. Room kinds stay within today's enum until phase 2.
    4. 4

      Room shapes

      A shape per role: hand-made templates (rotated and mirrored) or procedural shapes (pillared halls, octagons, braided mazes, cellular-automata caverns). Sockets sit at the midpoint of each side. A 0-1 BFS carves the fewest walls needed to connect all four sockets, and floor pockets nobody can reach are filled in.

      New: generation/roomShapes.ts. loadContent keeps rejecting templates whose sockets do not connect.
    5. 5

      Carve

      Stamp each room into its cell with random jitter. Join neighbours socket to socket with a corridor that jogs once inside the gap. Rooms in adjacent cells always have at least 4 tiles between them.

      Replaces carveCorridor. The BFS router can stay as a fallback.
    6. 6

      Populate

      Doors first, in the locked room's own socket. Then lights, spawns, the exit, monsters from a threat budget, key holders, loot and chests by role, traps, events, then puzzle plates and ballast stones. Corridor torches go every 7 tiles, filtered by the biome's light level and omens.

      Grows populate. Instances still come from dungeonBuilder.ts.
    7. 7

      Validate

      Flood-fill the real tiles. With every lock closed, the boss room and vaults must be unreachable. Collecting keys and solving puzzles in any reachable order must reach the exit. Every room must be reachable at the end, and every entity must stand on floor. On failure, retry with attempt + 1, up to 40 times.

      New: generation/validate.ts, shared with tests/server/generation.test.ts.
    Layout archetypes

    Five flows

    A flow sets the shape of a run before any room is chosen. Each thumbnail below was drawn by the generator with that flow forced, with the flow graph on. Numbers mark the critical path. Dashed amber edges are key locks, dashed violet edges are seals, and blue edges are loops.

    Themes

    Four biomes

    A biome is a content file. It lists the monster roster with threat costs, trap and puzzle pools, a boss pool, weights for room kinds, a light level and tint, a loot table and room names. Cinder Depths uses only content that ships today, so phase 1 needs no new art. The other three are proposals; each needs a monster set, wall and floor art, and a light tint.

    The bottom of every run

    Bosses

    Every boss is a monster with boss: true, which already seals the exit until it falls (bosses.ts). Each design below adds one co-op mechanic that a single player can't easily handle alone. Each arena style is a room shape in stage 4.

    Loot and pacing

    Risk pays, and the curve rises

    Loot is placed according to what a room costs to clear. Threat comes from a budget, so depth and party size raise the danger without hand-tuning each room.

    Threat budget per room

    budget = base[role]
       × (1 + 0.5 × depth)        // 0 at the entrance, 1 at the boss
       × (0.6 + 0.2 × partySize)  // 0.8× solo … 1.4× for four
       × omen.budgetMult          // Restless Dead: 1.4

base = { hall 5, cavern 5, maze 3, vault 3,
         trap 2, secret 2, arena 2 + waves, puzzle 1 }
cost = { cinder-slime 1, ash-imp 1,
         cinder-spitter 2, slag-charger 3,
         elite or keeper 4, boss 12 }

    Pacing rules

    • No trap room right after the entrance, so the party gets its bearings first.
    • No room kind twice in a row on the critical path.
    • A camp at about 60% of the path, as a breather before the climb to the boss (from Gungeon's breather rooms).
    • Arenas only from three hops deep.
    • The boss is always last, and the exit is a dead end behind it.
    • The walk to the exit takes about 1.5 to 3 minutes of pure walking (p10 89 s, median 129 s, p90 181 s over 2,000 seeds). Fighting and exploring fill the rest of the 15 to 20 minute target.

    Moving this into the game

    Porting guide

    The reference generator sticks to the repo's conventions so the port is mostly translation. It uses the same mulberry32 stream as random.ts and never calls Math.random. It works in authored tiles, and scaleDungeon runs afterwards unchanged. Light radii are world units. The map alphabet is still only # and ., so phase 1 needs no renderer or tile changes. Its output has the same shape as BuiltDungeon, plus a graph for debugging.

    Protocol changes, in the order they are needed

    Each one is a breaking change and bumps PROTOCOL_VERSION from 36. Follow the protocol-contract-change skill.

    Invariants to sweep in tests

    Extend the 120-seed sweep in tests/server/generation.test.ts to every party size from 1 to 4 and every flow. The reference passed 6,000 seeds (1,500 per party size) and catches both mutation tests: a key moved inside the boss room, and locks never placed.

    1. Every spawn, monster, loot, chest, trap, plate, altar and exit is on open floor.
    2. With every lock closed, no tile inside the boss room or a vault is reachable from spawn.
    3. Collecting keys and solving puzzles in any reachable order opens a route to the exit.
    4. Every room is reachable once all locks are open.
    5. No puzzle needs more plates held than the party has players plus ballast stones.
    6. Every key is reachable before the door it opens. This follows from 2 and 3, and is worth naming in the test.
    7. The same seed and party size give byte-identical tiles and ids.
    8. The boss and exit rooms have no extra connections: the exit is a dead end behind the boss.
    9. Generation stays under 5 ms per run. The reference takes 1.6 ms in Node, and npm run bench:tick should not change, because generation happens once per run, not per tick.

    Opinion checkpoints

    Decisions for the owner

    Each comes with a recommendation. None of them blocks phase 1 except the first.

    Should the generator lay rooms out on a grid of cells with sockets at the side midpoints, instead of free placement?
    RecommendYes. The grid is what makes branches and loops cheap and safe: corridors stay inside the gap between two rooms, so they can't cross rooms or bypass locks. Most of the games studied here use a grid.
    Should one run stay one floor, or should the exit offer a stair down?
    RecommendOne floor through phase 4. Then add a Descent option at the exit: go up and bank your loot, or go down one depth with harder budgets, better tables and a new biome. That is the roguelite push-your-luck, but it changes how a run saves (#122), so it should wait.
    Should the map alphabet grow (water, chasms, bridges)?
    RecommendNot yet. Everything in phases 1 to 3 is done with entities (doors, switches, plates, traps) on # and .. AGENTS.md calls a new tile character a coordinated content, collision, protocol and renderer change, so it should earn its place later, with pit rooms.
    Should the omen be shown before the run or found inside?
    RecommendBefore, on the quest board. An informed "do we go in under Smothering Dark?" is a good conversation for friends in the hub. Biome and flow can stay hidden until the party is inside.
    How should solo and duo parties handle puzzles built for four?
    RecommendBallast Stones plus scaling. The generator never asks for more plates than players, and spawns a carryable stone for each missing hand. Group-only content (Split Seal, Pale Twins) is filtered out below two players.
    Should biome be per run or per depth?
    RecommendPer run for now, with Cinder Depths only in phase 1. If Descent ships, switch to per depth so going down feels like going somewhere new.
